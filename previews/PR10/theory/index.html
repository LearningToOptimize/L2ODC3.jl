Theory

This page states the two methods as optimization problems, fixes the notation, derives the derivatives and step sizes the implementation uses, and records where the package departs from the papers. Equation numbers in parentheses refer to the papers:

  • [DC3] P. L. Donti, D. Rolnick, J. Z. Kolter. DC3: A learning method for optimization with hard constraints. ICLR 2021. arXiv:2104.12225
  • [FSNet] H. T. Nguyen, P. L. Donti. FSNet: Feasibility-Seeking Neural Network for Constrained Optimization with Guarantees. 2025. arXiv:2506.00362

The parametric problem

For parameters $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (JuMP parameters, the columns of P) both papers consider (eq. (1) of [DC3] and of [FSNet])

\[\min_{y \in \mathbb{R}^n} \; f_x(y) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad g_x(y) \le 0, \qquad h_x(y) = 0,\]

with $h_x : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^{n - m}$ (the problem is not overdetermined) and $g_x : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^{n_\text{ineq}}$.

Representation. The package reads every constraint of the JuMP model as a row $\ell_i \le F_i(y, x) \le u_i$ in the L2OCore.dual_layout order (a AbstractProblemForm). Rows with $\ell_i = u_i$ are the equalities, $h_{x,i}(y) = F_i(y, x) - \ell_i$. The remaining rows with a finite bound are the inequalities. For an inequality row, the signed distance to its interval,

\[r_i(y) = F_i(y, x) - \Pi_{[\ell_i, u_i]}\big(F_i(y, x)\big),\]

satisfies $r_i^2 = \mathrm{ReLU}(g_{x,i}(y))^2$ for one-sided rows and handles two-sided rows ($\ell_i \le F_i \le u_i$) as one row. Its gradient in $F$ is $\nabla_F \tfrac12 \|r\|^2 = r$, the projection residual. Every violation and every correction direction in the package is computed from this one quantity (inequality_residual, total_violation). Variable bounds are rows like any other.

Maximization problems are handled by minimizing $s \cdot f$ with $s =$ objective_sign.

DC3

Equality completion (Section 3.1 of [DC3])

Split $y = (z, \varphi_x(z))$, where $z \in \mathbb{R}^m$ are the independent variables predicted by the network and $\varphi_x(z)$ are the dependent variables defined by

\[h_x\big([z;\ \varphi_x(z)]\big) = 0 .\]

Differentiating this identity gives the implicit-function-theorem Jacobian (eq. (3) of [DC3])

\[\frac{\partial \varphi_x(z)}{\partial z} = -\big(J^h_{:,\varphi}\big)^{-1} J^h_{:,z},\]

and backpropagation needs only the vector-Jacobian product (eq. (4)):

\[\bar z = \bar y_z - \big(J^h_{:,z}\big)^{\top} w, \qquad \big(J^h_{:,\varphi}\big)^{\top} w = \bar y_\varphi .\]

The same argument gives $\bar x = -(J^h_{:,x})^\top w$ for the parameters, which NewtonCompletion also returns.

Affine equalities $A_E y + B_E x + b_E = \ell_E$ (LinearCompletion): with $D$ the dependent and $Z$ the independent columns,

\[y_D = -A_{E,D}^{-1}\big(A_{E,Z} z + B_E x + b_E - \ell_E\big),\]

which is precomputed as one affine map $Y = K_z Z + K_x X + k_0$. Completion is then a single batched matrix product, and its Jacobian $K_z$ is exact.

Nonlinear equalities (NewtonCompletion): damped Newton's method on $y_D$ for each instance, with the derivatives above.

Choice of the dependent variables. Any $D$ with $A_{E,D}$ (or $J^h_{:,D}$ at a reference point) nonsingular is valid. [DC3] draws random subsets until the block is nonsingular. Here select_dependent uses QR with column pivoting, first on $A_E^\top$ to drop redundant equality rows and then on the kept rows to pick well-conditioned columns. Dropped rows are checked to be consistent for every parameter value: $[A_E\ B_E\ b_E]$ restricted to them must lie in the row span of the kept rows. Otherwise the problem is infeasible for some $x$ and an error is thrown.

Inequality correction (Section 3.2 of [DC3])

With the reduced inequality violation

\[v(z) = \big\| \mathrm{ReLU}\big(g_x([z;\ \varphi_x(z)])\big) \big\|_2^2 ,\]

one correction step is $z \leftarrow z - \gamma \Delta z$ with

\[\Delta z = \nabla_z v(z) = \Big(\frac{\partial y}{\partial z}\Big)^{\!\top} J^g(y)^\top\, 2\,\mathrm{ReLU}(g_x(y)), \qquad y = [z;\ \varphi_x(z)],\]

followed by re-completion (reduced_violation_gradient, inequality_correction). As in the reference implementation (momentum $0.5$, Appendix B of [DC3]), the steps use heavy-ball momentum, $v_{k+1} = \mu v_k + \Delta z_k$ and $z_{k+1} = z_k - \gamma v_{k+1}$, which equals the reference update $s \leftarrow \gamma \Delta y + \mu s$, $y \leftarrow y - s$. $\rho^{(t)}$ denotes $t$ steps; training uses $t_\text{train}$ and testing $t_\text{test}$ (Algorithm 1 of [DC3]).

For affine equalities, re-completion gives exactly the paper's step $y - \gamma[\Delta z;\ (\partial\varphi/\partial z)\,\Delta z]$. For nonlinear equalities the paper's step keeps the equalities only to first order; re-completion keeps them to the Newton tolerance (see Deviations below).

For affine constraints $\ell \le A y + B x + b \le u$, the reduced violation is

\[v(z) = \operatorname{dist}^2\big(\hat G z + \hat H x + \hat g_0,\ [\ell_I, u_I]\big), \qquad \hat G = A_I K_z,\]

a convex function whose gradient $2 \hat G^\top r$ is Lipschitz with $L = 2 \|\hat G\|_2^2$. This holds because the projection residual is 1-Lipschitz (firm non-expansiveness of the projection). The default step is $\gamma = (1 - \mu)/L$ (default_step_size times $1 - \mu$). For $\mu = 0$ this is $1/L$, the step of the descent lemma. For $\mu > 0$ it lies in the range where the averaged heavy-ball iterates converge on convex $L$-smooth functions (Ghadimi, Feyzmahdavian & Johansson, 2015). The paper instead fixes $\gamma = 10^{-7}$ for its QPs, "as most methods went unstable for higher correction learning rates" (Appendix B.1 of [DC3]).

On the paper's convergence statement. Section 3.2 of [DC3] states that, for problems with linear constraints, the correction process converges, without a precise statement. Convergence of the corrections below gives one: with a step $\gamma \le 1/L$ and a nonempty feasible set, the violation goes to zero without momentum, and the averaged iterates converge with momentum.

Training loss (eq. (2) of [DC3])

\[\ell(\hat y) = f_x(\hat y) + \lambda_g \big\|\mathrm{ReLU}(g_x(\hat y))\big\|_2^2 + \lambda_h \big\|h_x(\hat y)\big\|_2^2, \qquad \hat y = \rho_x^{(t_\text{train})}\big([z;\ \varphi_x(z)]\big),\quad z = N_\theta(x),\]

averaged over the batch (soft_loss). The gradient flows through the completion (eq. (4)) and through the unrolled corrections.

The paper's equation uses squared norms. The authors' reference code instead uses plain norms $\|\mathrm{ReLU}(g)\|_2$ and $\|h\|_2$, with $\lambda_g = \lambda_h = 5$ (total weight 10, equality fraction 0.5). DC3(; penalty = :norm, λ_ineq = 5, λ_eq = 5) reproduces that loss; the default is the paper's equation.

Differentiating unrolled corrections

The correction direction is itself a gradient, so differentiating $\hat y(\theta)$ needs second derivatives. The package never asks the AD system for an inner gradient (no nested AD). Instead:

  • affine problems: $\Delta z = 2 \hat G^\top r$ is explicit array code, and any first-order AD differentiates through it;

  • nonlinear problems: $\Delta z$ is assembled from two primitives with ChainRules rules that use exact Hessian-of-the-Lagrangian products (MathOptInterface.eval_hessian_lagrangian_product):

    • $G = J_y F(y)^\top W$ (constraint_vjp): $\bar W = J_y F\, \bar G$ and $\bar{(y, x)} = \big(\sum_i W_i \nabla^2 F_i\big) [\bar G; 0]$;
    • $w = (J^h_{:,D})^{-\top} r$: $\bar r = s := (J^h_{:,D})^{-1} \bar w$ and $\bar{(y, x)} = -\big(\sum_i w_i \nabla^2 h_i\big)\, \hat s$, where $\hat s$ is $s$ placed on the dependent coordinates.

    With these, $\Delta z = \big(G - J^{h\top} w\big)_Z$ for $G = J^{g\top} 2r$, and $w$ solves the transposed system with right-hand side $G_D$.

All of these are checked against finite differences in the test suite.

FSNet

FSNet predicts all variables $y_\theta(x)$ and minimizes the total violation (eq. (3) of [FSNet])

\[\phi(s; x) = \|h_x(s)\|_2^2 + \|\mathrm{ReLU}(g_x(s))\|_2^2 = \|F(s, x) - \Pi_{[\ell, u]}(F(s, x))\|_2^2\]

by gradient descent from the prediction (eq. (4)), $s_0 = y_\theta(x)$, $s_{k+1} = s_k - \eta\, \nabla_s \phi(s_k; x)$ (feasibility_seeking), truncated to $K$ iterations. Derivatives flow through the first $K' \le K$ iterations only. The remaining ones add a constant offset (truncated unrolled differentiation, Section 5 of [FSNet]). The training loss is (eqs. (5)–(6), with the optional early-stage penalty of Section 5)

\[F = f_x(\hat y) + \frac{\rho}{2}\|y_\theta(x) - \hat y\|_2^2 + \rho_\phi\, \phi(y_\theta(x))\, [\phi(y_\theta(x)) \ge Q] .\]

For affine constraints, $\phi$ is convex with $L = 2\|A\|_2^2$ (lipschitz_constant), and the default step is $\eta = 1/L$.

Convergence of the corrections

Completion. If $A_{E,D}$ is nonsingular (the dependent-variable selection above enforces it), the affine completion satisfies $h_x(\hat y) = 0$ up to round-off for every proxy output (Section 3.1 of [DC3]). For nonlinear $h$, the completion satisfies the equalities to the Newton tolerance whenever Newton's method converges, which is the case for $h \in C^2$ with $J^h_{:,D}$ nonsingular near the root and a start in its basin. Under the same nonsingularity, and $h \in C^1$ near the completed point, the implicit function theorem gives eqs. (3)–(4), so the vector-Jacobian product above is the derivative of the completion map.

Descent. If $\nabla v$ is $L$-Lipschitz and $0 < \gamma \le 1/L$, a correction step without momentum decreases the violation by the descent lemma,

\[v(z - \gamma\nabla v(z)) \le v(z) - \gamma\big(1 - \tfrac{L\gamma}{2}\big)\|\nabla v(z)\|^2 \le v(z) - \tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla v(z)\|^2 .\]

No convexity is needed. The same inequality holds for FSNet's $\phi(s_{k+1}) \le \phi(s_k) - \tfrac{\eta}{2}\|\nabla\phi(s_k)\|^2$ with $0 < \eta \le 1/L$. With momentum the iterates are not monotone in general.

Smoothness for affine constraints. Write $v(z) = \|\hat r(z)\|^2$ with $\hat r = \hat F - \Pi(\hat F)$ and $\hat F = \hat G z + c(x)$. The map $F \mapsto F - \Pi(F)$ is 1-Lipschitz, so

\[\|\nabla v(z) - \nabla v(z')\| = 2\|\hat G^\top(\hat r(z) - \hat r(z'))\| \le 2\|\hat G\|^2\|z - z'\| ,\]

which is the constant $L$ used for the default step.

Rates for affine constraints. The function $v$ is convex (a squared distance to a convex set, composed with an affine map) and $L$-smooth. Gradient descent with $\gamma = 1/L$ gives $v(z_k) - v^\star \le L\|z_0 - z^\star\|^2 / (2k)$. If the reduced feasible set $S$ is nonempty, $v^\star = 0$. By Hoffman's error bound for polyhedra, $v(z) \ge c\, \operatorname{dist}(z, S)^2$ (quadratic growth), which for convex functions is equivalent to the Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality (Karimi, Nutini & Schmidt, 2016). With PL constant $\kappa$ this gives the linear rate $v(z_k) \le (1 - \kappa/L)^k v(z_0)$. The same argument applies to FSNet's $\phi$ with $A$ in place of $\hat G$, and gives $\phi(s_k) \le (1 - \kappa/L)^k \phi(s_0)$, the rate of Theorem 1 of [FSNet] (where the PL constant is called $\mu$). With momentum $\mu$ and step $(1 - \mu)/L$, the averaged heavy-ball iterates converge at rate $O(1/k)$ (Ghadimi, Feyzmahdavian & Johansson, 2015). The paper's instances are feasible by construction (eq. (6) of [DC3]).

Agreement with the reference code and with solvers. For affine equalities, the completion, the reduced gradient (G_eff, h_eff), the momentum steps and the loss coincide with the formulas of the authors' code. Starting from the independent part of a solver optimum, completion and correction reproduce that optimum, and the soft loss there equals $f^\star$. Descent on the DC3 loss reaches feasible points within 1–2% of HiGHS on QPs and LPs, and returns to Ipopt's local optimum on a nonlinear problem.

Deviations from the papers

  • Dependent-variable selection: pivoted QR instead of random subsets (deterministic and better conditioned). Users can pass dependent.

  • Nonlinear correction: re-completion after each $z$-step instead of the first-order step on $\varphi$, so the equalities hold to the Newton tolerance after every step. This is identical for affine equalities.

  • Second-order terms through the nonlinear correction are exact, from MathOptInterface Hessian-of-the-Lagrangian products rather than autograd of hand-written Jacobians as in the reference code.

  • FSNet inner solver: gradient descent, the setting of the paper's theory. The experiments in [FSNet] use L-BFGS with line search; this is a planned extension.

  • FSNet truncation: derivatives through the first $K'$ of $K$ iterations (as stated in Section 5 of [FSNet]), with the later iterations entering as a constant offset.

  • Jacobian formed explicitly for affine equalities: [DC3] recommends vector-Jacobian products instead of forming $\partial\varphi/\partial z$. For affine equalities the Jacobian is constant, so $K_z$ is formed once and completion becomes one batched matrix product (GPU-friendly). Nonlinear completion uses VJPs as recommended.

  • Newton completion is damped (backtracking on $\|h\|_\infty$). Appendix C.3 of [DC3] reports that solving for all variables with Newton's method "is sometimes unstable" on AC-OPF and splits the completion into a Newton part and a closed-form part. Such problem-specific completions plug in through AbstractCompletion.
